MAIN FINDINGS

1. The H14 history effect now has a concrete early MLP carrier. Reciprocal
activation-donor experiments identify L0 neurons 1091, 3419, 6535 and 4798 as a
conditionally sufficient/necessary group for the large L1 primary pulse in
eight controlled contexts. This is not a unique minimal circuit or a claim
about downstream loss. Neuron1091 alone induces much of the pulse, but is not
strictly necessary for every residual negative write.

2. Alternate L1 writers are behaviorally relevant after neuron1710 is removed.
Deleting the selected negative-writing group makes two controlled The-source
prefixes worse mainly in the 1710-deleted background. These small panels do not
establish a universal backup set.

3. For source0 newline187, removing positive canceling writers 2476/2637/7845
partially rescues full-model loss after1710 deletion. Critically, removing their
axial opposition alone does not reproduce the rescue. Their axial and off-axis
components interact strongly. The useful source cannot be reduced to simply
making coordinate1764 more negative.

1. EARLY CAUSAL CHAIN: H14 TO L0 MLP TO L1 MLP

Four archived prose probes hold q128=newline187 fixed and compare p64=space209
with p64=newline187, or remove only the L0/H14 q128-to-p64 attention edge. Sixteen
fresh two-layer passes reproduce the previous normalized L1 inputs: native U/R
states are bitwise identical; edge/H0 controls differ by at most2.65e-5. All
positions before q stay bitwise unchanged under the edge interventions.

An independent head14-only replay matches the actual changed pre-M0 residual
within8.20e-8. The previous and current newline have identical L0 value vectors,
because position enters Q/K through RoPE rather than being added to embeddings.
Nevertheless, in this fixed distance64 setup, the prior newline receives about
249.614 times the self-newline attention weight. Its attention mass is .928-.954.
After its edge is removed, self-newline receives only .052-.084; most remaining
weight goes to other content. This positional ratio is specific to the tested
token pair and relative distance, not a claim about every previous newline.

A query-local H14 K/V factorial in the p64-space recipient identifies the first
MLP gate switch. Supplying the newline value without its key leaves source mass
at .00074-.00119 and L0 neuron1091 strongly active. Supplying the newline key
without its value redirects attention but also leaves1091 active. Joint K+V
changes flip1091's gate negative and move its activation from approximately
-10 to +.53-.61. All other heads and query positions stay in the recipient
world in this arithmetic intervention; it is not a global key replacement.

In the native history-suppressed versus edge-blocked probes,1091's gate moves
from about -2.03..-2.41 to +4.44..+5.25; its up factor stays negative. The gate
switch produces the activation sign switch. Swapping only the up factor does
not reproduce it, while swapping only the gate produces an even larger negative
activation. Thus the first gate switch is distinct from the later backup pair's
joint gate/up switch.

The L0-neuron output decomposition uses the centered L1 gate/up rows as linear
features of the pre-L1-MLP residual. It splits the gate/up changes into A0, M0,
A1 and a symmetric normalization interaction. For n/s, the identity is
Delta(n/s) = Delta(n)*mean(1/s) + mean(n)*Delta(1/s).
M0 then splits exactly by individual neuron activation changes. Floating-point
closure error is below1.28e-6 for components and4.93e-6 for neurons. These are
signed arithmetic allocations, not percentages of mediation or performance.

Neuron1091 is the largest M0 contributor to the1710 gate change and to both
alternate-writer up changes in all four discovery probes. The selected top-four
group for the1710 gate is1091/3419/6535/4798, frozen before causal donor tests.

3. ALTERNATE L1 FACTORS ARE NOT COPIES OF THE PRIMARY FACTOR

In all four H14 discovery panels, the alternate pair3950/4786 has a combined
centered-axis write of about -.010..-.017 in the suppressed state. Supplying
only edge-world gates gives +3.26..+3.85. Supplying only edge-world up factors
gives +.042..+.054. Supplying both gives -8.29..-7.13. Both one-factor hybrids
have the wrong sign for the large negative pair write. This fixed-node
arithmetic interaction is not proof of natural-prefix reachability.

An explicit LayerNorm-domain check illustrates that caveat. The exact six
factor targets for edge-world primary1710 plus suppressed-world pair3950/4786
require minimum squared input norm2235.58-2349.03, exceeding the ideal ceiling
2048. The reverse combination is feasible. Matching only the primary's
edge-world factors while retaining the suppressed nullspace direction is also
feasible and gives a POSITIVE pair write. Therefore a strong primary does not
mathematically force negative backup writes. Only the exact six-factor target
combination is impossible, not every form of backup suppression.

For source187, the positive alternate trio2476/2637/7845 partly cancels existing
negative writers. Whole-trio deletion after1710 deletion changes source alignment
from-.00973 to-.65124. It now also produces a real, partial behavioral rescue.

The final four controls use the exact centered coordinate1764 unit axis.
The complementary raw vector includes the group's mean, making the two
components algebraically sum to its full output. They are vector-component
interventions, not neuron ablations. Axis-only source alignment improves to
-.43293 but does not reproduce the joint loss rescue; in DuBois it worsens loss.
Finite axis-by-complement interactions are-1.22371 and-.87321 NLL. These numbers
are factorial interactions, not mediation fractions. Joint deletion remains
worse than native, so this is not full recovery.

L2 source K/V and later source-attention trajectories are saved as diagnostics.
Matched source-token/treatment states and L2 keys are bitwise identical across
the two different suffixes. Their different losses thus reflect contextual use,
not different production at source0. No new K-only/V-only mediation experiment
is included in this cutoff; diagnostics alone do not establish which projection
causes the loss improvement.
